Jollof Special


🍅 Ghana Jollof Rice Ingredients


3 cups rice

5–6 fresh tomatoes

2 tablespoons tomato paste

1 large onion

2–3 cloves garlic

Ginger

Scotch bonnet or other pepper (optional)

Cooking oil

Chicken, beef, or fish

Chicken stock

Salt

Curry powder

Thyme

Bay leaves (optional)

Seasoning of your choice

Mixed vegetables (optional)

Method


Blend the base: Blend the tomatoes, part of the onion, garlic, ginger and pepper.

Cook your meat: Season and cook your chicken or meat. Keep the stock.

Fry the onion: Heat oil in a large pot and fry chopped onions.

Add tomato paste: Add the tomato paste and fry for a few minutes.

Add blended tomatoes: Pour in the blended mixture and cook until the water reduces and the sauce becomes thick. This is important for a rich Jollof flavour.

Season: Add salt, curry powder, thyme, bay leaves and other seasonings.

Add stock and water: Add your meat stock and enough water for the rice.

Add washed rice: When the sauce is boiling, add the washed rice and stir.

Cook: Cover tightly and cook on low-to-medium heat. Add small amounts of water if necessary.

Steam until soft: When the rice is cooked, fluff it with a fork.

Serve with chicken, fried plantain, salad or shito 😋.

Waakyee


🫘 Waakye Ingredients


3 cups rice

1–2 cups black-eyed beans or brown beans

Dried millet leaves (waakye leaves)

Water

Salt

Method


Wash and soak the beans for a few hours or overnight if possible.

Put the beans in a large pot and boil until they begin to soften.

Wash the waakye leaves and add them to the beans.

Add enough water and allow the leaves to boil with the beans. The leaves give waakye its brownish colour and distinctive flavour.

Wash your rice and add it when the beans are partly or fully softened.

Add salt.

Add enough water to cook the rice and beans together.

Cover and cook on low heat until the rice and beans are soft.

Remove the waakye leaves before serving.

Popular things to serve with waakye 🍽️


Shito

Wele

Gari

Spaghetti

Fried plantain

Egg

Fish

Meat stew

Salad

Tip: For authentic waakye, don't add too much water at once—just enough to cook the rice without making it mushy.
